# What Yellow tells us about Red and Blue

Yellow is the closest official comparison for an English Red/Blue rule. It can identify a repair, a new design choice, or an old defect that survived another release. The strongest case for a Core repair combines the comparison with a specific failure in Red/Blue and a test for the intended result. The [binary comparison](yellow-data-diff.json) covers the machine compatibility data for all 151 species, all 165 move records and the type matchups.

| Question | Current preset choices | Developer controls |
|---|---|---|
| Can Charizard learn HM02 locally? | Minimal keeps stock eligibility. Core adds Fly. | Override the single permission independently. |
| How does a Ghost attack interact with Psychic? | Minimal retains 0×. Core uses 2×. | Select the row and match the Gym hint to it. |
| What types are Karate Chop, Gust and Sand-Attack? | Core uses Flying Gust. The other two moves stay Normal. Minimal retains all three Normal types. | Select move types individually. |
| Can an NPC walk back south after wandering too far north? | Core repairs the one-way movement error. Minimal keeps cartridge movement. | Broader changes to wandering range remain a separate design choice. |

These comparisons concern the English presets. The Japanese games add historical evidence and require no additional build target.

## Scope of the retail data check

A fresh Yellow build matched the English retail SHA-1. The [table comparison tool](../tools/compare_yellow_data.py) read it alongside hash-matched Red and Blue, using the symbols from each build. Run `python3 tools/compare_yellow_data.py /path/to/built/pokered /path/to/built/pokeyellow` to reproduce the [recorded differences](yellow-data-diff.json). It handles Mew’s separate base-data record in Red/Blue.

| Data checked | Yellow compared with English Red/Blue |
|---|---|
| 151 species × 55 machine permissions | Seven additions and one removal. Charizard gains Fly. Butterfree, Venonat and Venomoth gain Flash. Diglett, Dugtrio and Kabutops gain Cut. Mewtwo loses Pay Day. |
| 165 six-byte move records and 82 explicit type-chart entries | Identical in all three ROMs, including the order of the chart entries. The records cover animation ID, effect ID, power, type, accuracy and PP. Their effect routines can differ between games. |
| Starting and level-up moves | Thirteen species have different starting moves and 23 have different level-up lists. Selecting Yellow machine permissions need not select any of these. |
| Evolution records, five base stats, species types, growth rate and base experience | Identical for all 151 species. Scripts can still add special rules, as with Yellow’s partner Pikachu. Catch-rate data changes for Dragonair and Dragonite. |

An independent set of [18 Red/Blue turn results](yellow-behavior-checks.json) also compares Lick and Night Shade against a pure Normal target and Karate Chop and Gust against a pure Ghost target. At level 45, Lick did zero damage against Psychic, while Night Shade dealt 45 against both Psychic and Normal. Both Normal-type attacks failed against Ghost and damaged a pure Water control. All used the normal battle menu with disposable test parties. These observations establish the cartridge outcomes used to test author options.

## Charizard and Fly

Charizard is Fire/Flying in English Red, Blue and Yellow. Its Red/Blue machine list includes Cut and Strength. Yellow adds Fly. The Red/Blue item handler uses each species’ machine list to decide whether a machine can teach it. Other Flying types, including Butterfree and Gyarados, also lack Fly in Red/Blue. Typing alone does not determine eligibility. Gold/Silver and FireRed/LeafGreen confirm Charizard's later access. The evidence establishes an official revision to this one species' move choices. The original omission could have been an oversight. No contemporaneous statement establishing that was located. [Red/Blue Charizard](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/data/pokemon/base_stats/charizard.asm#L6-L22) · [Yellow Charizard](https://github.com/pret/pokeyellow/blob/e89ead154b9968aa50eed9328ff2b38b6c194382/data/pokemon/base_stats/charizard.asm#L6-L22) · [Red/Blue teaching check](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/engine/items/item_effects.asm#L2198-L2234) · [Butterfree](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/data/pokemon/base_stats/butterfree.asm) · [Gyarados](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/data/pokemon/base_stats/gyarados.asm) · [Gold/Silver](https://github.com/pret/pokegold/blob/ef0201d8daf47e8b3ea1518eacf890f37d4cd5e8/data/pokemon/base_stats/charizard.asm#L24) · [FireRed/LeafGreen](https://github.com/pret/pokefirered/blob/037335f4c725d7c9aecdac87066f2002b4bd7e14/src/data/pokemon/tmhm_learnsets.h#L122-L149)

The same Japanese guide that lists a Ghost weakness gives Charizard a complete machine list on printed page 120. It ends with HM01 and HM04. HM02 is absent, and the move list identifies HM02 as Fly. This supports treating the original teaching list as deliberate. Core adopts Yellow’s subsequent permission as a design choice. [Charizard’s entry, printed p. 120](https://archive.org/details/pokemon-illustrated-book-of-pocket-monsters-character-art-book-encyclopedia-poke/page/n121/mode/1up) · [HM02 entry, printed p. 108](https://archive.org/details/pokemon-illustrated-book-of-pocket-monsters-character-art-book-encyclopedia-poke/page/n109/mode/1up)

A Charizard that can learn Fly can fill a party's field-travel slot and gain a Flying-type battle attack. This changes meaningful choices even with the original badge, HM and four-move limits. Core includes this single permission under D50. The original Japanese Pokédex explicitly describes Charizard in flight. The broader Yellow compatibility selection should identify its other entries, including Mewtwo **losing** Pay Day. Keep that selection independent of Yellow's level-up learnsets. Changing teaching eligibility must never erase an existing move received by trade, imported in a supported save or supplied by the hack. Test HM02's party list, refusal, teaching, move replacement, battle use and field use with and without the Thunder Badge. [Core decision](core-rule-precedents.md) · [Fly field checks](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/engine/menus/start_sub_menus.asm#L123-L150) · [Checked learning differences](yellow-data-diff.json)

**A one-permission build works on the compact engine.** In a disposable Red build and a Blue build from the implementation pin, adding FLY to Charizard’s machine list changed one gameplay byte (file offset 0x38484, from 0x26 to 0x2E) and the low byte of the global ROM checksum. No engine code or data layout changed. Twenty direct executions of the game’s machine-eligibility routine covered stock Red, Blue and Yellow and both edited builds. Fly was allowed for Charizard only in Yellow and the two edited builds. Charizard retained Cut and Strength in all five, and Gyarados could not learn Fly in any. This proves the isolated author edit and teaching check. Field use and the full teaching UI are still release tests for a supported option. ## Ghost and Psychic

**There is real conflict in the contemporary evidence.** In Japanese Red/Green, a Saffron trainer answers a question about advantages over Psychic by naming ghosts and bugs. Japanese Yellow keeps that text and the Ghost immunity. English Red/Blue also names both. English Yellow changes the hint to mention only bugs. In each of the checked Gen 1 charts, the Ghost attack row explicitly lists Psychic with `NO_EFFECT`. Bug against Psychic uses `SUPER_EFFECTIVE`. This establishes a localization change in Yellow and rules out a missing chart entry as the immediate cause. An erroneous value entered into the original table remains possible. [Japanese Red/Green text](https://github.com/Narishma-gb/pokegreen/blob/953f41b34108621b2bf13c3b1e53abfc9c3e5aec/scripts/SaffronGym.asm#L241-L245) · [Japanese Yellow text](https://github.com/Narishma-gb/pokeyellow-jp/blob/f282e72ae26232790fdb780aa5a5db7ec8ebf572/scripts/SaffronGym.asm#L306-L310) · [Japanese Yellow chart](https://github.com/Narishma-gb/pokeyellow-jp/blob/f282e72ae26232790fdb780aa5a5db7ec8ebf572/data/types/type_matchups.asm#L66-L79) · [English Red/Blue text](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/text/SaffronGym.asm#L134-L139) · [English Yellow text](https://github.com/pret/pokeyellow/blob/e89ead154b9968aa50eed9328ff2b38b6c194382/text/SaffronGym.asm#L134-L138) · [English Red/Blue chart](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/data/types/type_matchups.asm#L65-L80)

The Japanese *An Illustrated Book of POCKET MONSTERS* gives explicit contemporary evidence for the weakness. Printed page 106 labels rows as **attacking move type** and columns as **defending Pokémon type**. Ghost (Y) attacking Psychic (S) has the double-circle symbol for very effective. Ghost attacking Normal has the no-effect triangle. The chart also shows ordinary damage for Ghost against Ghost, which differs from the cartridge. The colophon credits Creatures with supervision, Tsunekazu Ishihara with guidance and Game Freak with supplying material. It lists the first edition as April 5, 1996. The copy inspected is the sixth printing, dated February 7, 1997. The chart is evidence that an early, closely connected publication taught a Ghost weakness. It cannot tell us who supplied or checked that entry, whether the first printing was identical, or why the implementation differs. [Japanese move chart, printed p. 106](https://archive.org/details/pokemon-illustrated-book-of-pocket-monsters-character-art-book-encyclopedia-poke/page/n107/mode/1up) · [Credits and printing history](https://archive.org/details/pokemon-illustrated-book-of-pocket-monsters-character-art-book-encyclopedia-poke/page/n145/mode/1up)

The US Red instruction booklet gives published evidence pointing the other way. On printed page 33, Ghost attack row 14 against Psychic column 11 has the green triangle, the same symbol as Normal attacking Ghost and Electric attacking Ground. Bug attacking Psychic and Ghost attacking Ghost have the black dot for a strong matchup. The booklet labels the triangle “BAD” and gives no numerical multiplier. It agrees with the direction of the shipped rule. Prima’s licensed guide, by comparison, recommends Ghost and Bug Pokémon against Psychic. Gold/Silver uses a super-effective entry. Stadium 2’s Academy describes Ghost attacks becoming effective against Psychic as a change between generations. [Original booklet, printed p. 33, PDF p. 35](https://www.videogamemanual.com/gameboy/Pokemon%20-%20Red%20Version%20%28USA%29.pdf#page=35) · [Prima guide review](historical-evidence.md#primas-guide) · [Gold/Silver chart](https://github.com/pret/pokegold/blob/ef0201d8daf47e8b3ea1518eacf890f37d4cd5e8/data/types/type_matchups.asm#L92-L95) · [Stadium 2 Academy text in the Stadium project’s notes](https://github.com/pret/pokestadium/blob/6995f32445e344c03f9b0c774eabcd157559f867/oldnotes/stadiumgs/text/academy/class.txt#L329-L336)

**The practical effect in Gen 1 is chiefly Lick.** Eighteen controlled turns in stock English Red, Blue and Yellow used the same three Ghost moves against pure Psychic and pure Water test targets. A level-45 user attacked after the target used Splash. X Accuracy was active to eliminate an ordinary miss. Lick did zero damage to Psychic in all three games and damaged Water in all three. Night Shade dealt 45 to both types in every game. Confuse Ray confused both. Night Shade follows the set-damage path and Confuse Ray applies a status. A blanket ban on Ghost moves against Psychic would break both. [Move records](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/data/moves/moves.asm#L112-L136) · [Damage dispatch](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/engine/battle/core.asm#L3131-L3155) · [Night Shade](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/engine/battle/core.asm#L4638-L4652)

Changing one cartridge to 2× while another uses 0× could cause a link battle to disagree on damage. That makes compatibility a plausible explanation for keeping the chart. We found no statement from the original team attributing the decision to link play. An early intention to make Ghost strong is credible. Core follows the early Japanese evidence and uses 2×. Minimal preserves the cartridge outcome. [Badge precedent](core-rule-precedents.md) A named community reference for faithful Gen 1 battles, Pokémon Showdown, also encodes the immunity. For the stock chart, use Yellow's English hint. For the 2× entry, test Lick's damage, feedback and same-type attack bonus, leave Night Shade and Confuse Ray on their own paths, and test matching rules on both ends of a link. [Showdown Gen 1 Psychic matchup](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/035c1a1f3c2ed286cda4c0d8623800699cd2c1c0/data/mods/gen1/typechart.ts#L110-L124) · [PokéRGB mechanics contract](../docs/05-mechanics-contract.md)

## Karate Chop, Gust and Sand-Attack

Red/Blue and Yellow explicitly assign Normal to all three. Stadium's surviving move-description records even call Karate Chop and Gust Normal-type attacks. Gold/Silver changes Karate Chop to Fighting, Gust to Flying and Sand-Attack to Ground. Gust also acquires its own move effect in Gold/Silver. These later rules have different histories. Pokémon Showdown's Gen 1 rules likewise retain all three Normal typings. [Red/Blue move rows](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/data/moves/moves.asm#L15-L41) · [Yellow move rows](https://github.com/pret/pokeyellow/blob/e89ead154b9968aa50eed9328ff2b38b6c194382/data/moves/moves.asm#L15-L41) · [Stadium descriptions for Karate Chop](https://github.com/pret/pokestadium/blob/6995f32445e344c03f9b0c774eabcd157559f867/oldnotes/stadium1/text/movedescriptions.txt#L5-L7) and [Gust](https://github.com/pret/pokestadium/blob/6995f32445e344c03f9b0c774eabcd157559f867/oldnotes/stadium1/text/movedescriptions.txt#L61-L63) · [Gold/Silver move rows](https://github.com/pret/pokegold/blob/ef0201d8daf47e8b3ea1518eacf890f37d4cd5e8/data/moves/moves.asm#L18-L44) · [Showdown Gust](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/035c1a1f3c2ed286cda4c0d8623800699cd2c1c0/data/mods/gen1/moves.ts#L362-L365), [Karate Chop](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/035c1a1f3c2ed286cda4c0d8623800699cd2c1c0/data/mods/gen1/moves.ts#L431-L435) and [Sand-Attack](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/035c1a1f3c2ed286cda4c0d8623800699cd2c1c0/data/mods/gen1/moves.ts#L711-L715)

The Japanese guide provides **different evidence for Gust and Karate Chop**. On printed page 107 it labels Gust H (Flying), with 40 power and 35 PP, and Karate Chop O (Normal), with 50 power and 25 PP. Page 108 labels Sand-Attack O (Normal). Page 106 defines both letter codes. The separate notes column uses the Japanese phrase for an ordinary attack on both Gust and Rock Throw, so the type column is the one to read. A Flying Gust has direct contemporary support, even though both English Game Boy releases and the Stadium description say Normal. A Fighting Karate Chop has clearer evidence as a Gen 2 revision. Core selects Flying Gust and keeps Karate Chop Normal. A developer can change either type independently. [Type key](https://archive.org/details/pokemon-illustrated-book-of-pocket-monsters-character-art-book-encyclopedia-poke/page/n107/mode/1up) · [Gust and Karate Chop](https://archive.org/details/pokemon-illustrated-book-of-pocket-monsters-character-art-book-encyclopedia-poke/page/n108/mode/1up) · [Sand-Attack](https://archive.org/details/pokemon-illustrated-book-of-pocket-monsters-character-art-book-encyclopedia-poke/page/n109/mode/1up)

Test a Fighting-type user's Karate Chop against a Normal target and an early Pidgey's Gust against a Bug target. These expose changes in the same-type bonus or the matchup. Pidgey's Normal/Flying typing gives it the same-type bonus on either version of Gust. Keep Karate Chop's original high-critical behavior in both tests. A move-type option should leave Gust's original behavior against Fly/Dig intact unless the developer also selects the later targeting rule. Sand-Attack has zero power and reduces accuracy. Test the status effect against ordinary and airborne targets independently of any displayed move type. ## A narrow Yellow repair for wandering NPCs

The earlier CAT-039 report bundled a proposed roaming radius with a real movement fault. Red/Blue initialize each roaming displacement to 8. A north step subtracts one. When attempting a south step, the routine adds one and rejects results below 5. Four north steps leave 4, from which a south step can return to 5. Five north steps leave 3, from which a south step is refused because the result would be 4. The same test can block horizontal movement when the vertical displacement is low. Yellow removes precisely the refusal. The other collision, screen and coordinate checks still run. [Initialization](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/engine/overworld/movement.asm#L439-L450) · [Red/Blue check](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/engine/overworld/movement.asm#L638-L670) · [Yellow check](https://github.com/pret/pokeyellow/blob/e89ead154b9968aa50eed9328ff2b38b6c194382/engine/overworld/movement.asm#L607-L639)

Celadon's north/south walker starts at (14,19). The six ground squares from there through (14,14) are all passable tile 0x5B. This was checked by decoding the 25-block-wide map and the original Overworld blockset. For position (x,y), read block (x÷2,y÷2) with integer division, then tile index 4 + 8×(y mod 2) + 2×(x mod 2) within that 16-tile block. A player watching from the side can leave this corridor clear. [Walker definition](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/data/maps/objects/CeladonCity.asm#L40-L45) · [Map dimensions](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/constants/map_constants.asm#L32) · [Map bytes](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/maps/CeladonCity.blk) · [Blockset](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/gfx/blocksets/overworld.bst) · [Passable tiles](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/data/tilesets/collision_tile_ids.asm#L11-L12)

Nine routine fixtures ran the original `CanWalkOntoTile` machine code in SameBoy 1.0.5 in DMG-B mode. Each used a walking sprite, a clear screen corridor and the real Overworld collision list. The fixture supplied directions, advanced the position after each successful step and read the carry flag and stored displacement at return. The experiment tests the movement rule and a feasible stock map corridor. It did not measure how often an NPC randomly takes that route in ordinary play.

| Stock game | Four north, one south | Five north, one south | Impassable water control |
|---|---|---|---|
| English Red | Allowed, ends at 5 | Refused, stays at 3 | Refused |
| English Blue | Allowed, ends at 5 | Refused, stays at 3 | Refused |
| English Yellow | Allowed, ends at 5 | Allowed, ends at 4 | Refused |

For Core, remove the condition that blocks movement back toward the starting position. Verify the behavior through the normal overworld loop on the Celadon map before shipping. Test horizontal movement at low vertical displacement, borders, walls, the player, another NPC, scripted walks, sight ranges and the separate fifteenth-sprite movement lookup. Keep the general choice of wandering radius outside the fix. Minimal preserves the original NPC paths.

## Evidence limits

The source links use exact revisions of reverse-engineered game code. The visually inspected Japanese guide scan covers printed pp. 106–108 and 120, with printing and credits on PDF p. 146. Its searchable PDF has SHA-256 `b86840da630ba369b0703cb2fe1210fc933070c803cc96b7b939ecf8b6adb212`. Original US booklet page 33 was inspected visually in the same scan identified by [E76](evidence.md#e76). The stock runtime observations use these English ROM SHA-1 values: Red `ea9bcae617fdf159b045185467ae58b2e4a48b9a`, Blue `d7037c83e1ae5b39bde3c30787637ba1d4c48ce2`, and Yellow `cc7d03262ebfaf2f06772c1a480c7d9d5f4a38e1`. The latest-game series, Japanese Yellow releases beyond the cited source, paired link effects and production preset builds are outside these tests.
