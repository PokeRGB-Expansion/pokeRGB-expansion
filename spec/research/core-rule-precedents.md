# Choosing Core rules when Gen 1 sources disagree

The [fixture results](core-refinement-checks.json) preserve the measured battle outcomes for both stock colors and both temporary builds.

Core adopts the Ghost weakness, Flying-type Gust and Charizard's HM02 permission. Minimal keeps all three English Red/Blue rules. Karate Chop and Sand-Attack remain Normal in both. Extended inherits Core and supports individual data overrides. The grounds for these decisions differ.

## What the badge precedent establishes

The original Japanese Red/Green dialogue provides evidence that predates the English translation. The Vermilion leader promises Speed, the Fuchsia leader promises Defense, and the Cerulean badge house repeats both assignments. The English dialogue, US instruction booklet and later FireRed/LeafGreen calculation agree. The shipped Red/Blue calculation transposes the two bonuses. Core follows the better-supported assignment even though badge boosts affect battle results. | Decision | Evidence that determines the placement |
|---|---|
| **Ghost attacks deal 2× to Psychic in Core.** | The early Japanese guide explicitly labels Ghost *attacks* as very effective against Psychic. The original Japanese Gym hint also names ghosts and bugs. Gold/Silver implements that weakness, and all four sampled hack families adopt it. This meets the badge precedent for acting on likely intent. There is more disagreement here than for the badges. The cartridge table explicitly encodes immunity. The US **Red** booklet marks the matchup “BAD”, English Yellow mentions only bugs, and Japanese Yellow still mentions both. Core follows the earlier specification of the matchup. How the contrary table entry arose and why it survived Yellow remain unknown. | **Gust is Flying in Core.** | The same Japanese guide gives the move a Flying type, alongside the same power and PP as the cartridge. Gen 2 uses Flying. Gen 1 cartridges and a Stadium description instead identify it as Normal. The early contradiction is specific enough for a Core type correction. Keep power 40, nominal accuracy 100, PP 35, and the ordinary attack effect. Gold/Silver's special airborne-target behavior is a separate rule. | **Charizard learns HM02 in Core.** | Its **original Japanese Pokédex entry** says it flies on its wings up to 1,400 metres above the ground. The same game's HM table excludes Fly. The Japanese guide also omits the permission and Yellow later grants it. Core adopts that narrow official Gen 1 improvement because it fits the creature and the existing field system. The evidence establishes its flight and its later HM eligibility. It leaves the original decision about carrying a trainer open. Test teaching, battle use, badge-gated field use, cancellation and a supported imported Charizard that already knows Fly. The checked Japanese guide is the February 1997 sixth printing of a work first published in April 1996. The wording of an uninspected earlier printing is uncertain. Nor does the guide establish every multiplier the cartridge should use. Each change above has its own corroboration and scope. Keep the ghosts-and-bugs clue for Core’s chart. Minimal can use Yellow’s English bugs-only advice. A hack that adds Dark or changes other matchups also needs advice consistent with its chosen chart. ## Measured effects in Red and Blue

Two disposable builds from `ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a` changed three game-data bytes. At ROM offset `0x3E557`, Ghost attacking Psychic changed from 0 to 20 (the Gen 1 encoding of 2×). At `0x3805D`, Gust changed from Normal to Flying. At `0x38484`, Charizard's machine byte gained the Fly bit. Every other differing byte belonged to the cartridge checksum. RGBDS 1.0.4 produced Red SHA-1 `d4b65c1bb4878206799abf7a2c4ff5989252d329` and Blue `9fa7b1a5f4d6d9e08b393883a5c1062e7805be1e`. These are research builds. The release will also need the other Core fixes.

**136 direct damage checks** used SameBoy 1.0.5 in monochrome Game Boy mode. For each of the two stock colors and two edited colors, the real type-adjustment routine ran with 40 damage before typing, for 17 move/target combinations and both possible attackers. Lick's user was Ghost/Poison, Gust's Normal/Flying and Karate Chop's pure Fighting. Repeated type slots represent a single type. The table shows HP damage after the same-type attack bonus and the chart, before the damage roll. **48 full battle turns** followed the normal FIGHT menu. Six situations ran for both attacking sides on both colors in stock and edited builds. Fixtures supplied level 45, types, moves and enough HP for both battlers to survive, with X Accuracy active. The target used Splash or Counter. Against pure Psychic, Lick dealt damage only in the edited builds, Night Shade dealt exactly 45 in every build, and Confuse Ray applied confusion in every build. Gust hit Ghost/Poison only after the edit. Against a Water target, stock Gust triggered Counter for double the damage received. Flying Gust dealt damage and Counter failed. Karate Chop triggered Counter for double the damage in all four builds. This follows Gen 1 Counter's existing requirement for a Normal or Fighting attack. Pidgey receives a same-type bonus on Gust in both rulesets because its types are Normal and Flying. ## Compatibility and release checks

Yellow’s link compatibility gives a technically plausible reason to keep an old battle chart. The two games exchange choices and run their battle calculations locally. A mixed pair with different charts can disagree about HP and bout results. The same concern applies to Gust’s type and Counter. Teaching Fly locally changes a permission that need not be consulted during a battle once the move is already known. This explains why the two kinds of edit have different interoperability risks. We found no original developer statement assigning that motive to Yellow's team. The earlier tests and the prototype are single-console checks. Require two-peer results for supported modified battles and test trading separately. [Original Yellow features](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/n02/dmg/apsj/index.html) · [Stock link move exchange](https://github.com/pret/pokered/blob/ba9c0568f64ae602fadda577a08c66639f93082a/engine/battle/core.asm#L3007-L3044)

When the revised chart is enabled, run the effectiveness text and sound tests against it. For custom Normal/Psychic, immunity must win in both type orders, including the message. Confirm that trainer scoring reads the configured chart while retaining its existing first-match logic. Test Gust on a target using Fly or Dig so the newer type does not accidentally import Gold/Silver's airborne exception. Check that Fly requires a learned move, the Thunder Badge, a suitable map and an available destination. Verify these same cases with individual author overrides, with animations off and in any supported link configuration.
